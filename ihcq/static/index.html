    Help

    What the numbers mean

    H-score = 1·%1+ + 2·%2+ + 3·%3+ over all cells, 0–300. Allred = proportion score (0–5) + intensity score (0–3). % positive = cells at or above the 0/1+ cut. DAB area % = tissue pixels above the 0/1+ cut, over the tissue footprint (holes filled). Mean DAB OD = optical density averaged over tissue — under Beer–Lambert, OD is linear in chromogen amount, RGB brightness is not.

    Each cell's DAB is read in a compartment: the nucleus (Ki-67, ER, PR, p53), a ring outside it (HER2, PD-L1) or a wider band (cytoplasmic markers). The per-cell statistic is the mean by default; the 90th percentile is more faithful for punctate or membranous staining.

    Workflow that holds up

    1. Score a negative control and a strong positive. Look at the overlays — do the outlines sit on nuclei?
    2. Set the pixel size (or nuclear radius) so the finder is at the right scale.
    3. Calibrate the cut points on controls, save the profile, use it for the whole series.
    4. Read the QC flags on every result. They are there to stop a number leaving the room unearned.

    What it will refuse to pretend

    If the second stain is eosin, the field is H&E and DAB scores are marked invalid — nuclear counts and density still stand. If the field has no glass, the white point is assumed and flagged; give it a value measured on a blank area. If nuclei look far larger or smaller than assumed, it says so. Melanin is brown and is not DAB; it reads "unrecognised".
